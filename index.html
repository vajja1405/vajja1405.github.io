Medication evidence · Full-stack AI

Drug Interaction Agent

Public demo

A medication-label review app that brings source passages and coverage gaps into one view, with a separate RAG chatbot for research.

Choose medications, inspect every pair, and follow the evidence back to its label. The interface distinguishes direct label mentions, shared ingredients, and incomplete source coverage.

Engineering decision

Keep deterministic label review separate from generated explanations. A missing mention is displayed as a coverage result, never as proof that a combination is safe.

Research and source-review tool, not a clinically validated interaction checker. It does not establish patient-specific safety or recommend changes to medication.

Architecture, evaluation, and scope

The public label-review path uses terminology matching and label text without an LLM or a generated severity score. Label sampling can miss relevant evidence, and a direct name mention may include a negated statement. Users must read the source passage.

The separate research chatbot combines retrieval and classification stages with an LLM explanation on the ordinary uncached API path. The classifier module uses isotonic calibration with three-fold cross-validation. Caching reduces repeated work; it does not establish an equivalent reduction in overall operating cost.

The authored 24-pair regression set achieved 24/24 severity matches on its evaluated path. It excludes the final LLM explanation and is not a full RAGAS evaluation or clinical validation. The expanded app also has 41 integration tests and six static-demo tests; those check software behavior, not medical accuracy.

Clinical NLP · Retrieval · Evaluation

ClinIQ

NSF NRT · 4th place

An exploration of substance-use relevance detection and documentation-review workflows, comparing rules, embeddings, and LLM-assisted retrieval.

The corpus contains 52,184 public patient-written drug reviews. A 600-review comparison made the tradeoff concrete: the embedding method recovered every proxy-positive case but flagged almost the entire sample.

From model score to review workload

The analyst’s next decision is how many alerts can be reviewed and how many signals may be missed. A workload planner makes prevalence and review-time assumptions explicit, with a downloadable scenario for pilot planning.

What the comparison taught me

More sophisticated models do not automatically produce a better workflow. Rules had the strongest F1 here; LLM + RAG was more precise but missed many proxy-positive examples.

Documentation gaps and the revenue opportunity

The business exploration asked whether documentation review could surface potentially missing ICD-10 information for a qualified reviewer. A separate synthetic claims workflow explored how CC/MCC classification could affect hospital MS-DRG reimbursement.

The $27.5M–$60M figure is a rough scenario: 10,000 admissions × an assumed $2,750–$6,000 payment difference. It is not measured revenue, verified recoverable revenue, or a forecast. Real impact requires chart review, valid coding, a demonstrated change in DRG assignment, and payment verification.

Hospital MS-DRG economics are different from Medicare Advantage risk adjustment. A hospital payment increase also does not automatically create savings for a health insurer.

Voice AI · Evaluation · Aug–Sep 2026

Voice-Agent QA Harness

Engineering challenge

A test harness for an engineer deciding whether a phone-agent change is ready for a pilot. It places synthetic-patient calls and makes failures reviewable through transcripts, audio and scenario grades.

What the user does next

Inspect the failed scenario, distinguish a test artifact from an agent defect, fix the right component, and replay the same case. Appropriate human escalation counts as a successful outcome when the scenario requires it.

Evidence before a release decision

15designed scenarios
12historical recorded calls
  1. Exercise the workflowEveryday tasks, rule compliance, memory and safety probes.
  2. Inspect failure evidenceTranscript grading, audio analysis and manual review.
  3. Check regressionsReject incomplete suites, unsafe results and synthetic preview grades.

Built for the Pretty Good AI engineering challenge. These are synthetic-patient tests, not a patient deployment. Offline bridge and grading tests do not replace a new live replay; no improvement in patient outcomes is claimed.

Engineering changes and the next useful experiment

Playback tracks generated audio separately from phone playback, bounds truncation on interruption and discards late interrupted chunks. Grading validates its schema and supporting quotes, selects the latest scenario capture, and records source provenance. Preview runs cannot overwrite historical evidence.

A useful pilot would compare matched scenarios before and after a change, including repeated runs, task completion, appropriate escalation, latency and reviewer time. The existing 12 calls do not complete the 15-scenario release suite.
